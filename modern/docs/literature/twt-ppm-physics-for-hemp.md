## 0. Scope, method and verification policy

**Question.** The HEMP thruster is a Thales Electron Devices spin-off of travelling-wave-tube
(TWT) periodic-permanent-magnet (PPM) focusing. Our geometry generator is literally called
`generate_twt_inspired_ppm_stack` (`cft_revival.geometry.generators`). What does the
sixty-year-old PPM literature predict for such a stack, and do our recorded fields (96
accepted L1a sweep-v2 designs, the P2 divergent-exit FEM field) and orbit findings
(zero reflections in v4, 11-55 % reflections and mu non-conservation in the geometry
screening) agree with it?

**Method.** Five questions (sections 1-5), each with the sources, what the theory
predicts, and a "check against our fields" subsection fed by
`scripts/ppm_axis_field_check.py` (read-only on every artifact; tables A-G2 in section 6
are its verbatim output, also stored as `scripts/ppm_axis_field_check.output.json`).
Section 7 is the expectation -> observation -> agreement -> implication table; section 8
lists honest gaps; section 9 is the bibliography.

**Verification tags** (every entry in section 9 carries one; nothing unverifiable is
cited):

- `[T]` full text read (PDF via ERPS `electricrocket.org`, publisher page, or Google
  Patents full text);
- `[R]` Crossref record resolved by DOI **and** abstract read (OpenAlex/Crossref);
- `[C]` Crossref record resolved by DOI (title, authors, venue, year, volume, pages
  confirmed), abstract not deposited;
- `[B]` book: publisher / library record confirmed (Artech House, CERN library,
  Cambridge University Press DOI, archive.org catalogue);
- `[P]` patent: Google Patents record read (number, inventors, assignee, priority,
  filing and grant dates).

DOIs were resolved through `api.crossref.org/works/<DOI>`; two DOIs I "knew" from memory
were wrong and were replaced by the search results (Mendel 1954 is
`10.1109/JRPROC.1954.274515`, not `...274680`; Sterrett-Heffner 1958 is
`10.1109/T-ED.1958.14326`, not `...14351`). A regex cross-check of every `[n]` in the
prose against section 9 was run before commit.

**Notation.** `L` = stage pitch (magnet-to-magnet spacing, the distance between
successive field reversals); the field period is `2L`. `kappa = pi/L`. `r_w` = channel
(dielectric) inner radius; `r_m` = magnet inner radius; `x_w = pi r_w / L`. `I_0, I_1` are
modified Bessel functions. `b_k` are the axis harmonic amplitudes. "Cusp" is used in the
literature sense fixed by blocker 3: an axis null `(0, z_c)` whose separatrix meets the
wall at `z_c`.

The HEMP inherits the **hardware pattern** (ring stack, alternating polarity, iron in the
circuit in at least the early hardware) and the **cell language** (one magnetic cell per
collector stage). It does **not** inherit the two quantities that define a TWT PPM design:
the period-to-beam-wavelength matching and the Brillouin field. Those are replaced by a
mirror-ratio criterion that the TWT literature never needed (section 4.2).

### 1.3 Check against our fields

- Geometry v1.1 is a faithful TWT-pattern stack: each stage is one magnet ring
  (axial fraction 0.48-0.72 of the pitch) followed by a pole-piece ring filling the rest of
  the pitch, alternating polarity, no taper. The pole pieces are present as regions with
  `relative_permeability 4000` but the L1a field is the vacuum field of the two
  equivalent current sheets per magnet (`magnet-0k-inner/outer-sheet`, opposite sign) -
  the pole-piece bands are source-free vacuum (mu_r = 1) in every accepted sweep-v2 map.
  The P2
  divergent-exit field (adaptive FEM) is the only recorded field with iron.
- The stage count (3, 4, 5 in 26 / 45 / 25 of the 96 designs) matches the HEMP-T's three
  magnet rings [36,37] and Keller et al.'s downscaled HEMPT with "up to three" cusps [39];
  the DCFT has three ring cusps [45].
- Our channel radii (1.4-2.2 mm) are micro-thruster scale (Keller 2015 [39], Brandt 2016
  [42]), a factor 5-20 below HEMP-T 3050 channel diameters of 22.5-48 mm [37]; the
  dimensionless numbers that the PPM theory cares about (`r_w/L`, `r_m/L`, `x_w`) are
  reported in section 6 and used throughout.

## 2. PPM field theory

### 2.1 Sources

- Mendel, Quate, Yocom 1954 [1] `[R]`: the founding paper; periodic permanent-magnet
  structure of N cells is lighter by ~N^2 than the equivalent uniform magnet; focusing
  "comparable to results obtainable with a uniform field"; "stop bands" where the beam is
  lost. Mendel 1955 [3] `[C]` and Harker 1955 [4] `[C]` extend the theory (shielded and
  partially shielded cathodes).
- Chang 1955 [2] `[R]`: periodic fields focus like a uniform field "if a proper choice of
  the value of the period with respect to the rms value of the field is employed"; the
  required period becomes impractically small for high-power tubes (hence combined
  "complementary" fields).
- Buck 1957 [5] `[R]`: non-sinusoidal periodic fields ("encountered in many typical
  periodic focusing structures"); minimum ripple when "the rms value of the magnetic
  field equal to the Brillouin value".
- Sterrett, Heffner 1958 [6] `[R]`: design charts giving "the peak magnetic field required
  for periodic focusing in terms of the electron-beam parameters, the magnet and
  pole-piece dimensions required to obtain this peak field, and the resultant weight".
- Siekanowicz, Sterzer 1956 [7] `[C]`: an early 100 W S-band PPM TWT (RCA).
- Schindler 1966 [8] `[R]`: RCA design procedure; predicted peak axial fields "within five
  percent" of the built structures, "applicable to any magnet material whose
  demagnetization curve is known", with "a section on the design of the soft-magnetic
  pole pieces ('shims')".
- Leupold, Clarke 1987 [9] `[R]`: field enhancement / bulk reduction by radially
  magnetised cladding of the PPM stack; compares "more conventional methods of field
  enhancement".
- Santra, Kumar, Balakrishnan 2013 [10] `[R]`: long-periodic (multiperiodic) PPM
  structures by permeance / magnetic-circuit analysis, checked against FEM and
  measurement - the modern engineering treatment of axial-profile shaping.
- Textbooks: Pierce 1950 [17], Pierce 1954 [18], Gilmour 1994 [15], Gilmour 2011 [16],
  Carter 2018 [19] (all `[B]`: records verified; used here for the standard results
  only, no page numbers are quoted).

### 2.2 What the theory says

**Field of an infinite stack.** In the source-free bore the field is a gradient of a
harmonic scalar potential. For an axis field with half-period `L` (reversal spacing) and
odd symmetry about the reversal planes,

    B_z(0, z) = sum_{k odd} b_k cos(k kappa (z - z_0) + phi),   kappa = pi / L,

the unique Laplace extension is

    B_z(r, z) = sum_k b_k I_0(k kappa r) cos(k kappa (z - z_0) + phi),
    B_r(r, z) = sum_k b_k I_1(k kappa r) sin(k kappa (z - z_0) + phi),
    psi(r, z) = r A_phi = sum_k b_k (r / (k kappa)) I_1(k kappa r) cos(...),

(div B = 0 uses `I_1' + I_1/x = I_0`; curl B = 0 is immediate). The paraxial form
`B_r = -(r/2) dB_z/dz` is the `I_1(x) -> x/2` limit; it under-predicts `|B_r|` by the
factor `(x/2)/I_1(x)` (0.86 at x = 1.1, 0.76 at x = 1.5).

**Cusps.** The reversal planes are the zeros of the axis series. For the fundamental
alone, `z_c(n) = z_0 + (n + 1/2) L - phi/kappa`: one cusp per pitch, midway between
magnet centres, i.e. at the pole-piece (gap) planes. With only odd harmonics
`cos(k pi/2) = 0` for every odd k, so `B_z(r, z_c) = 0` at every radius: the separatrix
of the axis null is the exact plane `z = z_c` and the wall field at the cusp is purely
radial (angle to the wall normal = 0). Even harmonics (unequal magnet lengths, end
effects) tilt the separatrix.

**Peak field.** On the axis `|B_z|` peaks at the magnet centres; at the pole-piece bore
`B_r` peaks at the pole planes. Sterrett-Heffner and Schindler design the pole-piece
inner radius, thickness and gap so that the on-axis field has the required peak and is
close to sinusoidal; Buck [5] treats the departures.

**Radial growth and harmonic attenuation.** Harmonic k grows off axis as
`I_1(k kappa r)`. Between the pole/magnet bore `r_m` and the channel wall `r_w` the
k-th harmonic is attenuated, relative to the fundamental, by

    a_k = [I_1(k x_w) / I_1(k x_m)] / [I_1(x_w) / I_1(x_m)],   x = kappa r.

For a wall well inside the bore this factor is small (section 2.4: median 0.14 for k = 3
over our 96 designs), so the field seen at the wall is the fundamental whatever the pole
pieces do at the bore. This is also why TWT beams (r << r_m) never see the harmonics.

**Expected wall-field ratios for a stack of pitch L and wall radius r_w** (fundamental
only):

- wall field at the cusp / axis peak:  `B_r(r_w, z_c) / b_1 = I_1(x_w)`;
- wall field at the cusp / wall field under the magnet centre:
  `I_1(x_w) / I_0(x_w)` (< 1 for all x: along the wall the cusp is a |B| **minimum**);
- the wall field exceeds the axis peak only for `x_w > 1.52` (r_w/L > 0.48); it reaches
  1.5 x the axis peak at r_w/L = 0.62, 4 x at 0.96, 10.6 x at 1.30 (values of `I_1`).

**Mirror ratio along a field line.** The flux function above gives the field line
through a launch point `(r_0, z_mid)` at a magnet centre:
`r I_1(kappa r) cos(kappa (z - z_mid)) = r_0 I_1(kappa r_0)`. It reaches the wall at
`cos(kappa (z_w - z_mid)) = r_0 I_1(kappa r_0) / (r_w I_1(x_w))`, i.e. **before** the cusp
plane for every `r_0 > 0`, and `|B|` along it falls from `b_1 I_0(kappa r_0)` toward the
wall (section 6, Table E). A pure-fundamental PPM bore contains no magnetic mirror on
the way from a magnet centre to the wall; the |B| maxima are the magnet centres.

**End effects.** A finite stack of N magnets has N - 1 interior reversals plus one
reversal beyond each end (N + 1 axis nulls): the end magnets have a neighbour on one side
only, their axis peak is lower, and the interior cells are wider than the pitch (Table
B). Sterrett-Heffner and Schindler handle the ends with modified end pole pieces.

### 2.3 Expected numbers for our design class

With `r_w = 1.4-2.2 mm` and `L = 3.8-6.5 mm` (96 accepted designs):

- `x_w = 0.72-1.55` (median 1.10); `I_1(x_w) = 0.38-1.03` (median 0.64; 2 designs above
  1); `I_1/I_0 = 0.34-0.61`;
- `r_m / L = 0.38-1.00`; third-harmonic attenuation bore -> wall relative to the
  fundamental `a_3 = 0.035-0.51` (median 0.14);
- cusps at the stage gaps `z_gap(n) = (c_n + c_{n+1})/2` for an infinite stack, displaced
  outward from the stack centre in a 3-stage stack (end effect);
- wall-field angle at `z_c` ~ 0; wall cusp field 0.4-1.0 x axis peak; 0.34-0.61 x the
  wall field under the magnet.

### 2.4 Check against our fields (script Tables A-D)

- **Axis form.** The odd-harmonic cosine series fits the recorded axis `B_z` between the
  first and last stage centres to 2.7-4.9 % RMS of the peak for the L1a maps (8-14 axis
  samples per pitch) and 0.7 % for the P2 FEM field (120 samples per pitch). The fitted
  pitch equals the geometric pitch to 0.6 % (P2, 4 stages) and 1.9 % (v2-065, 5 stages)
  and is 11-15 % **larger** for the three 3-stage designs: the interior cell of a
  3-magnet stack is 1.09-1.11 pitches long (recorded nulls at -0.18/-0.25 mm and
  +0.18/+0.25 mm from the gaps, Table B). This is the PPM end effect, not a defect.
- **Harmonics on axis** are 0.01-0.02 (b_3/b_1, b_5/b_1) for the coarse L1a maps and
  1e-4 - 1e-3 for P2; the L1a values are at the aliasing floor of the 0.45 mm grid (the
  k = 5 wavelength is 1.5-2.6 mm) and must not be extended to the wall (Table C: they
  over-predict the wall `B_r` by 1.7-2.1 x because `I_1(5 x_w)` amplifies them 50-80 x;
  the fundamental alone reproduces the recorded wall maximum to 0-10 %, the paraxial
  formula to 4-14 %). Fitting the Bessel series directly to the recorded **wall** `B_r`
  (Table C2) gives `|b_3/b_1| = 0.2-0.5 %` and the wall `|B_r|` full width at half maximum
  is 0.96-1.16 x the pure-sinusoid value `2L/3`: **every recorded wall field, iron or no
  iron, is a single-harmonic PPM field at the wall**, as the attenuation factor predicts.
  The wall-fitted fundamental equals the axis-fitted one to 0.3-3.6 % (Laplace
  consistency of the recorded maps).
- **Cusp positions.** Recorded axis nulls sit at the fitted-series zeros to 0.05 mm
  (3-stage) and 0.13 mm (5-stage, where a single pitch cannot represent the end-effect
  gradient); the P2 nulls are within 0.03 mm of the gap planes (iron pole rings pin the
  reversal to the pole plane); the L1a nulls are 0.07-0.25 mm from the gaps. The v3
  separatrix wall intersections `z_c` agree with the axis nulls to 0.025 mm and the
  separatrix angle to the wall normal is 0.0002-1.6 deg (Table B), as required for an
  odd-harmonic field. The recorded wall field at `z_c` is within 0.02-3.9 deg of the wall
  normal (Table D).
- **Mirror ratios.** Wall field at the cusp / adjacent axis peak = 0.45-0.61 for all five
  fields (theory `I_1(x_w)` = 0.50-0.65; the recorded value is lower because the adjacent
  axis peak is the full finite-stack value while `b_1` is the fitted amplitude); wall cusp
  / wall field under the magnet = 0.37-0.52 (theory `I_1/I_0` = 0.41-0.49). The v3
  catalogue's `axis_mirror_ratio` (0.47-0.69 per cell) and `wall_mirror_ratio` (1.00 in
  every interior cell: the wall |B| minimum of a cell **is** the cusp) say the same
  thing. In every recorded field the wall cusp is the weak-field point of its cell.
- **Launch field lines** from the central magnet centre at 0.675 and 0.80 `r_w` reach
  the wall at 36-87 % of the way to the next cusp with `|B|` monotonically decreasing
  (B_wall/B_launch = 0.39-0.96; max along the line = the launch value) in all five
  fields (Table E).

Agreement with the PPM theory is quantitative on every point that the theory
addresses. The one place the fields differ from the infinite-stack formula is the end
effect, and it behaves as the TWT design literature says it does.

## 3. Electron dynamics in PPM fields

### 3.1 Sources

- Busch 1926 [20] `[C]`: conservation of canonical angular momentum in axisymmetric
  fields (Busch's theorem): `r^2 (dtheta/dt) = (e/2m)(B r^2 - B_c r_c^2)` in the paraxial
  form quoted by Carlsten et al. [11].
- Brillouin 1945 [21] `[C]`: the rigid-rotor laminar equilibrium of a beam in a uniform
  field (Brillouin flow); Pierce 1954 [18] `[B]` for the design use.
- Mendel-Quate-Yocom 1954 [1], Chang 1955 [2], Buck 1957 [5] (above); Carlsten et al.
  2005 [11] `[T]` (open access) restate the Mendel theory: with `T = 2 pi z / L_period`
  the beam-edge equation is `d^2 r/dT^2 + alpha (1 + cos 2T) r - beta/r = 0`, with
  `alpha = (L_period/2pi)^2 e^2 B_p^2 / (8 gamma^2 m^2 v_z^2)` and `beta` the space-charge
  term; balanced flow at `B_p = sqrt(2) B_Brillouin` ("the same rms focusing strength as
  for the uniform focusing case"); the emittance-dominated limit is a Mathieu equation
  whose solution "is stable if alpha < 0.66 or 1.72 < alpha < 3.76 or alpha > 6.10", and
  the space-charge-dominated beam has "roughly the same stability threshold". Hess and
  Chen 2002 [12] `[C]` and Chen, Pakter, Davidson 1997 [13] `[C]` are the modern
  Vlasov/rigid-rotor treatments of beams in periodic solenoidal fields; Basten and Booske
  1999 [14] `[C]` use "periodically cusped magnetic fields" for sheet beams.
- Non-adiabatic motion across a field reversal: Schmidt 1962 [23] `[R]` ("extremely
  nonadiabatic conditions ... if the particle passes through a region of vanishing
  magnetic field"; canonical angular momentum as the tool; "particle injection into
  cusped geometries"); Sinnis and Schmidt 1963 [24] `[R]` (electrons injected axially
  through a cusp are transmitted only "inside a 'loss disk' of radius
  rho_critical = (mv)/(qB_0)"; up to five reflections observed inside the cusp region);
  Roth 1964 [25] `[R]` (sinusoidally modulated current-sheet barrier; adiabatic vs
  non-adiabatic reflection of an ion beam); Leffel and Gray 1969 [26] `[R]` (spindle
  cusp; the adiabatic/non-adiabatic boundary flux surface `psi_c = (a lambda_0)^3` with
  `a = 3.5-4.4`, `lambda_0` the cyclotron radius of the total velocity; "the transition
  zone ... is about two-thirds of an electron cyclotron radius in width"; "the relative
  field variation encountered during one cyclotron period" at the boundary "is between 11
  and 19 %"; magnetic-moment fluctuations on successive traversals nearly uncorrelated ->
  random walk); Howard 1971 [27] `[R]` (analytic `Delta mu / mu` for linear and
  axisymmetric cusps, "within a few percent" of computed orbits); Rhee and Destler 1974
  [28] `[R]` ("A threshold energy for transmission through the cusp is found, and
  confirmed experimentally"; orbit off-centring from the finite width of the cusp
  transition); Cohen, Rowlands, Foote 1978 [29] `[C]` (mu jumps in mirrors); Northrop
  1963 [22] `[C]` (adiabatic theory); Haines 1977 [30] `[C]` (cusp containment review).

### 3.2 What the theory says

**Two regimes, one parameter.** Write the number of gyrations per field period at the
peak field as `2L / lambda_c`, `lambda_c = 2 pi m v_z / (e B_p)`. Then Mendel's
`alpha = (2L / lambda_c)^2 / 8`. TWT PPM design lives below the first stop band,
`alpha < 0.66` [1,11]: fewer than 2.3 gyrations per period (an illustrative 10 kV beam,
B_p = 0.15 T, 12 mm period gives alpha = 0.09, lambda_c = 14 mm, computed here, not
quoted). Each reversal is then a thin lens; the electron's transverse velocity
is set by Busch's theorem, not by mu conservation; there is no magnetic moment to
conserve and no mirror force to speak of (`v_perp << v_z`). **Reflection at a PPM cusp
does not occur in the TWT regime and the TWT literature does not discuss it**; what it
discusses is the parametric (Mathieu) instability of the beam envelope in the stop
bands, and the balance `B_rms = B_Brillouin`.

**The opposite regime.** A 5-25 eV electron in a 0.1-0.35 T stack makes 9-98 gyrations
per period (`alpha` = 10-1200, Table F). Here the guiding-centre picture holds in the
bulk of a cell (mu conserved, mirror force `-mu grad B`), and the field reversal is a
**local** non-adiabatic layer. Near the null `|B| ~ b_1 kappa d` (d = distance from the
null in the meridional plane), so `r_L(B) > d` inside the radius

    d_na = sqrt(m v / (e b_1 kappa)),

0.21-0.47 mm for our fields (Table F): the axis null is surrounded by a non-adiabatic
core of 3-12 % of the pitch. At the wall cusp the relevant number is
`epsilon = r_L(B_r(r_w, z_c)) / L_B`, `L_B = |B| / |dB_z/dz|` = `I_1(x_w) / (kappa I_0(x_w))`
for the fundamental (0.35-0.81 mm here). Leffel-Gray's boundary (11-19 % field change
per gyration, i.e. `2 pi epsilon ~ 0.11-0.19`, `epsilon ~ 0.02-0.03`) is exceeded by every
one of our launch classes (`epsilon` = 0.05-0.75, Table F): **every electron that reaches
a wall cusp in our fields is non-adiabatic there**, most strongly in the short-pitch,
low-field designs (v2-000, v2-032) and least in the long-pitch, high-field v2-065 and
P2.

**Transmission vs reflection at a reversal - the three limits.**

1. *Sharp cusp* (reversal width << r_L; Schmidt, Sinnis-Schmidt, Rhee-Destler): Busch's
   theorem gives a particle at radius r crossing from +B to -B an azimuthal velocity
   change `Delta v_theta = e B r / m`; it is transmitted only if `r < r_L(v) = m v / (e B)`
   (the "loss disk"), otherwise it is reflected by its own induced rotation. This is a
   non-adiabatic reflection with no mirror ratio in it.
2. *Adiabatic* (r_L << all scales): mu is conserved, the particle follows its field line,
   and is reflected where `|B| = B_launch / sin^2(pitch angle)` (1.13 x for 70 deg, 8.5 x
   for 20 deg) if such a point exists on the line before the wall; otherwise it hits the
   wall where the line does.
3. *Intermediate* (r_L ~ scale, our case): mu jumps by O(1) at each cusp transit
   (Howard, Leffel-Gray, Cohen et al.), the turning points move from transit to transit
   (random walk), and the reflection statistics are neither the loss-disk nor the
   loss-cone formula.

**Where the maxima are.** In a PPM bore (`x_w <~ 1.5`) `|B|` is maximal under the magnet
centres and minimal at the cusp planes at every radius (section 2.2). A mirror-confined
population therefore lives in "bottles" **centred on the cusps** and bounded by the
magnet centres, and the field lines leaving those bottles toward the wall do so near
(but before) the cusp planes. The HEMP picture of a cell centred on a magnet and bounded
by cusp mirrors [37,38] requires the wall cusp field to exceed the field in the cell,
which is the `rho > 1` condition of section 4.2 - not met by any of our fields.

### 3.3 Check against our findings (script Tables E-G2)

- **Zero reflections in v4 (P2 field).** The v4 launch cells were 3.5, 9.5, 15.5 and
  21.5 mm, i.e. 0.5 mm from the magnet centres 3, 9, 15, 21 mm, at r = 1.35 and 1.6 mm
  (0.675 and 0.80 r_w). Table E: from a P2 magnet centre at these radii the field line
  reaches the wall at 57-73 % of the way to the cusp with `|B|` falling to 0.69-0.75 of
  the launch value and no maximum along the line. Launched at the |B| maximum of its
  line, no electron can satisfy the mirror condition: zero reflections and 100 % wall
  loss in the interior cells is exactly what the PPM field predicts for **that launch
  position**. It is not evidence about the field topology.
- **11-55 % reflections in the L1a screening.** The screening launched at 1/8, 3/8, 5/8,
  7/8 of the straight span, positions that fall anywhere between a magnet centre and a
  null depending on the design (Table G2). The seven cells launched within 0.17 pitch of
  a magnet centre produced 0-1 reflections out of 128; the nine cells launched
  0.22-0.48 pitch from the nearest magnet centre produced 32-88 of 128 - including three
  cells where the launch `|B_z|` is still 0.72-0.77 of the stack peak. What decides is
  whether `|B|` rises by >= 13 % toward the nearer magnet centre before the field line
  reaches the wall, not the launch field value itself. At the
  turning points the recorded `|B|/|B|_launch` has median 1.08-1.14, i.e. the 70 deg
  mirror condition `1/sin^2(70 deg) = 1.13`; 51-82 % of the reflections satisfy the
  mirror condition, 6-32 % turn at `|B| < |B|_launch` (non-adiabatic `v_par = 0`
  crossings), and 83-100 % of the reflected orbits are the 70 deg class. The reflections
  are **mirror reflections toward the magnet-centre maxima**, occurring 0.14-0.27 pitch
  from the nearest null (Table G). Wall hits are 80-91 % closer to a null than to a
  magnet centre (median 0.12-0.18 pitch from the null): the cusps are where the field
  lines leave, not where electrons are reflected.
- **mu non-conservation (> 10 % in 60 % of v4 orbits; screening medians 0.11-0.47).**
  The per-design median mu variation orders exactly as the wall-cusp adiabaticity
  parameter at 25 eV: v2-000 (epsilon 0.75, mu median 0.43), v2-032 (0.63, 0.32), v2-068
  (0.37, 0.20), v2-065 (0.12, 0.11); P2 has epsilon 0.09-0.20 and a v4 mu median of 0.14.
  This is the Leffel-Gray / Howard regime, and it says the per-cusp-transit loss
  probability of the Kornfeld network cannot be a loss-cone number (blocker 2 of the
  reduced-model review reaches the same conclusion from the plasma side).
- **The "reflected" label.** orbit_mc's `reflected` termination is `v_par = 0` within a
  step. In a field whose direction rotates by 90 deg across a cusp, a non-adiabatic
  electron can register `v_par = 0` while continuing forward; the 6-32 % of reflections
  with `|B|_turn < |B|_launch` are candidates for this. Screening v2 should record `|B|`
  and the field-direction rotation at the turning point so that mirror reflections and
  direction-rotation events are separated.

## 4. Design rules

### 4.1 How TWT designers choose the PPM parameters

- **Period.** From the stability parameter: keep `alpha = (2L/lambda_c)^2/8` well below
  the first stop band at 0.66 [1,11]; Chang's remark that the required period
  becomes too small for high-perveance beams [2] is the origin of the "complementary
  field" and, later, of long-periodic structures [10].
- **Field amplitude.** `B_rms = B_Brillouin`, i.e. `B_p = sqrt(2) B_B` with
  `B_B = (m c / (e r_b)) sqrt(8 I / I_A)` (Carlsten's form [11]; `I_A` = 17 kA); minimum
  ripple [1,5]. For a 0.1 A, 300 V, 1.4 mm-radius "beam" this would be 0.045 T - quoted
  only to show that the rule has no analogue in a thruster (there is no laminar beam and
  no balance condition).
- **Pole pieces.** Inner radius, thickness and gap from the Sterrett-Heffner charts [6]
  or the Schindler procedure [8] (peak field within 5 % of prediction; soft-magnetic
  "shims"); non-sinusoidal shape effects per Buck [5]; end pole pieces for the finite
  stack.
- **Magnet material.** Schindler: any material with a known demagnetisation curve [8];
  space TWTs use Sm-Co (temperature-stable, high coercivity; Gilmour 2011 [16] `[B]`,
  Carter 2018 [19] `[B]`; no page-level quotation is made from either book). Bliss 1959
  (temperature compensation of PPM assemblies) exists in the record but was not read and
  is not cited.
- **Shunts, cladding, stray field.** Leupold-Clarke cladding [9]; Koch 2007 [37] report
  reducing the HEMP-T dipole moment from 95.5 to 5.79 A m^2 and the stray field at
  25 cm from 55 to 4.0 x 10^-5 T (DM9-1 -> DM10) by magnetic-circuit changes - a TWT
  magnetics concern (stray field on the delay line) inherited by the thruster (satellite
  integration).

### 4.2 HEMP magnet-stage studies

- Kornfeld 2007 [36]: DM3 (9 mm channel) -> DM3a (14 mm) -> 20 mm main-cusp diameter,
  because trajectory and PIC codes "suggested that the plasma electron confinement due
  to the mirror effect at the magnetic cusps was quite impacted and reduced by the low
  discharge channel diameter of only 9 mm, leading to high losses of kinetic energy at
  the discharge chamber walls in the cusp area"; the contours of field lines and channel
  wall "were more paralleled by increasing the length of the exit magnet ring compared to
  the upstream magnets"; "a series of different magnet configurations and small
  geometrical modifications were analyzed".
- Koch 2007 [37]: the design criterion. "High reflection of electrons in the cusp-mirror
  situation is achieved for a high ratio of the (predominantly radial) magnetic field
  strength at the inner discharge tube radius at the cusp middle plane to the
  (predominantly axial) magnetic field strength downstream the cusp." Table 1 of that
  paper: the ratio `rho` (anode / main / exit cusp) went from **1.53 / 1.70 / 3.07**
  (DM9-1) to **4.07 / 4.2 / 10.57** (DM10), "increased for all cusps by a factor of 2.66
  to 3.44", while the channel inner diameter went from 22.5 / 45 mm to 37 / 48 mm (main
  cusp / exit).
- Koch 2011 [38]: tapered channel and magnet system, variable magnet lengths; the exit
  cusp "formed by the stray field of the last downstream magnet"; "electron confinement
  is dominated by cusp mirror oscillations rather than by E x B drift".
- Keller et al. 2015 [39] `[C]`: downscaled HEMPT parametric study (per the DLR record
  quoted in the blocker-3 review: SmCo outer diameter 10-40 mm, cusp length 1-10 mm, up
  to three cusps).
- Lewerentz and Schneider 2023 [40] `[C]` (text read for the blocker-3 review):
  field-line length, mirror ratio `B_max/B_0` from a stage-midplane start point to its
  wall end point, confinement proxy `tau = l sqrt(R_m) r_0 e^{-r_0}`; `L/R = sqrt(6)` for
  a homogeneous on-axis field; magpylib [51] fields without iron.
- Matthias et al. 2020 [41] `[C]`: similarity scaling of the HEMP field with size;
  Brandt et al. 2016 [42] `[C]`: down-scaled HEMPT PIC.
- HIT multi-cusped field thrusters: Ma et al. 2015 [48] (variable magnet length), Hu et
  al. 2016 [49] (magnet length vs performance), Hu, Yu, Shen 2020 [50] (magnet-stage
  optimisation of a 5 kW thruster), Zhao et al. 2014 [46] (field strength), Liu et al.
  2015 [47] (mirror effects on the plasma distribution) - all `[C]`, cited for the
  existence and scope of the studies, not for numbers.

### 4.3 Which TWT rules transfer

| TWT rule | transfers to HEMP magnet-stage design? |
| --- | --- |
| period from alpha / stop bands | no - no beam; the pitch enters through `rho` and through the cell length that the plasma sees (Lewerentz L/R) |
| B_rms = B_Brillouin | no |
| pole pieces set the peak field and pin the reversal planes | yes - both patents and DM3a use pole shoes / Fe pole rings; Koch's rho increase was a "magnetic circuit" modification |
| Sm-Co magnets | yes (HEMP, DCFT [45], Keller [39]) |
| near-sinusoidal axis field | irrelevant to the thruster; the wall field is what matters, and at r_w/L < 0.5 it is sinusoidal regardless |
| uniform pitch | no - HEMP tapers and varies magnet length [36,38] |
| stray-field / dipole control | yes [37] |

### 4.4 Which assumptions of our parameterisation are supported

- **Stage count 3-5**: supported (three magnet rings in every HEMP-T generation [36,37];
  up to three cusps in the downscaled study [39]; three ring cusps in the DCFT [45]).
- **Alternating polarity, axially magnetised rings, one cusp per gap**: supported by
  every source and by our fields (N + 1 axis nulls for N magnets, cusps at the gaps to
  within 0.25 mm).
- **Uniform pitch and uniform channel radius**: TWT-like, not HEMP-like [36,38]. Our
  catalogue cannot express the tapered stacks and lengthened exit magnets that the HEMP
  designers used to align field lines with the wall.
- **Wall radius relative to pitch**: `r_w/L = 0.23-0.49` puts every design in the
  fundamental-only regime where the wall cusp field is 0.38-1.03 x the axis peak. Koch's
  `rho` = 1.5-3.1 (DM9-1) and 4-10.6 (DM10) would require, for a fundamental-only field,
  `r_w/L` = 0.62-0.87 and 0.96-1.30 respectively - a wall roughly one pitch from the axis,
  where the pole-bore harmonics are no longer attenuated and iron matters. None of the
  96 designs reaches `rho = 1.1`. The parameterisation samples the pitch and the radius
  but not the ratio that the HEMP literature optimises.
- **Magnet axial fraction 0.48-0.72** (the rest of the pitch a pole piece): a TWT-type
  split; the HEMP sources give no magnet/pole ratio, and in the L1a field the pole
  fraction is simply a gap.
- **Source strength as a free scale (0.75-1.3)**: harmless for topology (the field is
  linear) but it is not a magnet grade; `b_1` = 0.11-0.35 T on axis for the
  representatives.

## 5. Pole pieces and iron

### 5.1 What the literature says about iron in a PPM stack

- Sterrett-Heffner [6] and Schindler [8] design the pole pieces to obtain a required peak
  axial field from a given magnet; the "shims" concentrate the ring-magnet flux into the
  bore. Buck [5] measured what non-sinusoidal (pole-shaped) fields do to the beam. Leupold
  and Clarke [9] compare conventional field-enhancement methods with cladding. None of
  these papers quantifies, in a form transferable here, "how much sharper the cusp is with
  pole pieces than without": the TWT question was the on-axis peak and its sinusoidality,
  not the wall field at the reversal.
- What the Laplace structure of section 2.2 says instead: the pole-piece bore sets the
  harmonic content at `r_m`; the wall at `r_w < r_m` sees the k-th harmonic attenuated by
  `a_k` (0.035-0.51 for k = 3 across our catalogue, median 0.14). Iron can sharpen the
  wall cusp only when the wall is close to the pole bore (`x_w` large, `r_w -> r_m`), which
  is exactly the HEMP configuration (wall a few mm inside the pole rings of a 20-48 mm
  channel [36,37]) and not ours (magnets 0.85-1.7 mm behind a 1.4-2.2 mm wall, but
  `x_w` <= 1.55).
- The **magnitude** effect of iron is separate: pole pieces and a return yoke raise `b_1`
  for a given magnet (flux concentration and closure), which raises every field in the
  bore proportionally without changing any ratio. That is what the linear-vacuum L1a
  field cannot give and the L1b material solver or P2 must.

### 5.2 Does the HEMP-T use pole pieces?

- First patent: yes - "magnetic pole shoes PP ... deflect the magnetic flux generated by
  the permanent magnet PM ... arranged radially spaced from the ionization chamber" [31].
- DM3a (2002): yes - "Fe pole rings" in the cross-section [36].
- Later descriptions: "axially magnetized permanent magnet rings in opposite
  magnetization" [38], magnetic-circuit modifications to raise `rho` and cut the stray
  field [37]; Puca et al. 2024 [43] describe iron ring separators between the magnets
  (per the blocker-3 review).
- DCFT: a magnetic pole piece upstream of the SmCo rings [45]. Greifswald design tool:
  no iron (magpylib) [40,51].
- Conclusion: iron is present in the HEMP hardware lineage; the published PIC/design
  studies that omit it do so for tractability, not because it is absent.

### 5.3 Check against our fields

- **Iron vs no iron at our r_w/L.** The P2 field (iron pole rings and yoke, FEM) has
  wall harmonic content `b_3/b_1 = 0.2 %`, a wall `|B_r|` FWHM of 0.96 x `2L/3`, a Koch
  ratio 0.60-0.61 and a wall-cusp / wall-magnet ratio 0.48 - indistinguishable in shape
  from the bare L1a stacks (0.4-0.5 %, 0.98-1.16, 0.45-0.59, 0.37-0.52). At `x_w` = 1.05
  the iron does not sharpen the wall cusp measurably, as section 5.1 predicts. (P2 and
  L1a use different magnetisation authorities, so `b_1` itself is not comparable
  between them.)
- **What L1a gets right**: the topology (N + 1 axis nulls, planar separatrices, cusps at
  the gaps to <= 0.25 mm), the wall-field angle, the mirror-ratio class (`rho < 1`), the
  harmonic content at the wall, the end effects. **What it cannot get**: the absolute
  `b_1` of an iron circuit, flux leakage and stray field, and the `rho > 1` regime that
  only exists when the wall approaches the pole bore. For the current catalogue the
  linear-vacuum screening is adequate for topology and for the geometric access
  fraction; a material-aware field is required before any absolute field, mirror ratio
  or `rho`-based claim, and before any design with `r_w/L > 0.5`.
- **L1b** (`cft_revival.material_fields`, screening-only) is the right tool to bound the
  `b_1` shift for the four representatives with a declared iron spacer, as the blocker-3
  review already recommended; this document adds that the shift is expected in the
  amplitude, not in the cusp positions or the wall-field shape.

## 8. Honest gaps

- No page-level quotation is made from Gilmour [15,16], Pierce [17,18] or Carter [19]:
  their records were verified, their texts were not read for this document. Every
  formula in section 2 is derived here from Laplace's equation and checked numerically
  against the recorded maps; every stability statement is taken from the open-access
  Carlsten et al. paper [11] and the original abstracts [1,2,5].
- Koch's `rho` [37] is quoted as "radial field at the inner discharge tube radius at the
  cusp middle plane / axial field downstream the cusp"; the radius at which the
  downstream axial field is taken is not stated in the paper. Both readings (axis peak,
  wall field under the magnet) are reported in Table D and both give `rho < 1` here.
- The L1a axis maps have 8-14 samples per pitch; the `b_3, b_5` values in Table A are at
  the aliasing floor and only the wall-fitted values (Table C2) are trusted. A 2x refined
  re-solve (CPU, minutes per design) would settle the axis harmonics; it was not run
  because it is not needed for any conclusion above.
- The reflection-mechanism split (mirror vs direction rotation) uses `|B|` interpolated
  from the recorded structured map at the launch and turning points; it is an indicator,
  not a per-orbit proof.
- The topology columns of Tables B and D were first computed against the rejected v3
  bundle (`8cbcdbe6`) and then re-read from the accepted v3.1 bundle (`cec47f12`) after
  it landed on `feat/sota-foundation` during this work; the rows are identical, but the
  v3.1 catalogue is the only one a consumer may cite.
- The TWT-side "how much do pole pieces sharpen the cusp" question has no published
  number in a form that transfers; the attenuation argument is the honest answer, and
  our own iron-vs-no-iron comparison is limited to one P2 design with a different
  magnetisation authority.
- Bliss 1959 (temperature compensation of PPM assemblies), Rawls et al. 1967 (PPM
  focusing of convergent beams) and Sterzer-Siekanowicz 1957 (RCA Review design paper)
  appeared in the search results but were not read and are not cited.
