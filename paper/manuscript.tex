\begin{abstract}
Computational propulsion studies are difficult to update when the available
research artifact is an incomplete source snapshot rather than an executable
publication package. This paper develops an evidence-gated modernization
workflow around a preserved cusped-field-thruster design study. The current
contribution is reproducibility infrastructure: a technical audit of the
legacy MATLAB chain, a machine-readable equation ledger, a conservation-reduced
L0 operating-point model with Python and NVIDIA Warp cross-backend evaluations,
and a manifest-governed multi-fidelity optimization protocol.

\section{Introduction}

Cusped-field and highly efficient multistage plasma thrusters use permanent
periodic magnetic structures to shape electron confinement and acceleration.
Their coupled electromagnetic, plasma, and design-optimization behavior has
motivated reduced power-balance models, finite-element magnetic analyses,
surrogate-assisted optimization, and particle-in-cell (PIC) follow-up
\cite{Kornfeld2007,Muffatti2017,Fahey2017,Yeo2020}. Updating such a study is
not simply a matter of rerunning old scripts: the archived source can differ
from the publication narrative, external dependencies may be absent, and
numerical completion can be mistaken for physical validation.

This work therefore asks a narrower, auditable question: what can be claimed
now from the committed repository, and what evidence must exist before stronger
claims are admitted? The contribution is a modernization and reproducibility
architecture rather than a new best-performing thruster. It preserves the
legacy snapshot, separates code facts from publication claims, defines typed
fidelity and failure semantics, and requires every result section to be opened
by a committed manifest.

The paper contributes a traceable legacy audit, an equation-ledger-based L0
baseline, a manifest-bound current result, a preregistered and manifest-bound
collisionless test-particle electron wall-loss campaign in a qualified
finite-element field, three preregistered field-only topology-screening
studies admitted at their recorded outcomes (one accepted sweep and two
nulls), a preregistered robust multi-objective optimisation campaign of the L0
model admitted as optimiser evidence under a declared closure, an analytic
consistency result for the corrected four-cell power balance (no admissible root
for any positive interior cusp probability) admitted with a verification that
the checker recomputes, a preregistered collisionless test-particle wall-loss
screening across every accepted sweep design admitted as a screening dataset at
its label, a preregistered optimisation campaign of the L0 model over the
catalogue of those screened designs admitted as optimiser evidence under a
declared closure that identifies each design's screening wall-hit probabilities
with per-cusp survival factors, a preregistered screening of the literature
cusp definition across every screened design set admitted as a topology
screening whose cusps and cells are geometric properties of the field maps
(and whose lineage discloses a recorded rejection of its predecessor), a
preregistered geometry sweep that widens the wall-radius-to-pitch ratio into the
regime the HEMP design criterion requires and is admitted as a field-only
screening at its recorded hypothesis outcome, and a
constrained asynchronous multi-fidelity campaign policy. Each substantive result
is stated later through an exact registered claim or generated artifact rather
than authorized by a detached identifier.

\section{Literature lineage and scope}

Kornfeld, Koch, and Harmann described HEMP-thruster physics and a reduced
power-balance treatment in IEPC-2007-108 \cite{Kornfeld2007}. Muffatti and
Ogawa subsequently presented a small-scale CFT multi-objective design study at
ISTS 2017 \cite{Muffatti2017}. Related work coupled higher-fidelity magnetic
analysis and surrogate-assisted optimization \cite{Fahey2017}, and later used
PIC analysis to interrogate selected designs \cite{Yeo2020}. These papers
provide the scientific lineage, but they are not assumed to be bit-for-bit
oracles for the preserved source snapshot.

The optimization lineage combines classical constrained evolutionary
multi-objective search \cite{Deb2002}, scalarization-based Bayesian
optimization for expensive objectives \cite{Knowles2006}, noisy expected
hypervolume improvement \cite{Daulton2021}, and trust-region multi-objective
Bayesian optimization \cite{Daulton2022}. Multi-level emulation follows the
general principle that cheap and expensive codes must be related explicitly,
not pooled as exchangeable observations \cite{Kennedy2000}. The V\&V framing
distinguishes code verification, solution verification, model validation, and
prediction \cite{Oberkampf2002}; global sensitivity estimates, when eventually
admitted, must state their sampling assumptions and estimator
\cite{Saltelli2002}.

\subsection{Legacy, model, and campaign layers}

The architecture has four non-interchangeable layers:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item the preserved legacy MATLAB snapshot, treated as historical evidence;
  \item machine-readable equation, assumption, and provenance ledgers;
  \item evaluators that return typed numerical outputs, applicability
        diagnostics, residuals, and failures; and
  \item an optimization campaign that records designs, fidelity, seeds,
        objectives, constraints, cost, uncertainty, and immutable provenance.
\end{enumerate}

\EvidenceClaim{CLM-003}{L0 is an algebraic conservation-reduced operating-point
baseline with externally supplied charge-state, beam-current, axial-momentum,
cathode, and PPU inputs; it has no spatial mesh or geometric decision
variables.} The equation ledger excludes magnetic topology, channel geometry,
ionization, wall or thermal losses, cathode--plasma coupling, plume behavior,
erosion, and facility effects from the current model scope.

\subsection{Verified L0 equations and implementation}

For xenon mass flow $\dot m_{\mathrm{Xe}}$ and xenon particle mass
$m_{\mathrm{Xe}}$, the particle rate is
\begin{equation}
  \dot N_{\mathrm{Xe}} = \dot m_{\mathrm{Xe}}/m_{\mathrm{Xe}}.
\end{equation}
The represented charge-state fractions satisfy
$f_0+f_1+f_2=1$. For $z\in\{1,2\}$, the ideal directed ion speed is
\begin{equation}
  v_z = \sqrt{2 z e V_d/m_{\mathrm{Xe}}},
\end{equation}
subject to the documented nonrelativistic applicability gate. The beam current,
undiverged ion momentum flux, and axial thrust are
\begin{align}
  I_b &= e(\dot N_1+2\dot N_2),\\
  F_{\mathrm{ion}} &= m_{\mathrm{Xe}}
    (\dot N_1v_1+\dot N_2v_2),\\
  F_{\mathrm{axial}} &= k_{\mathrm{div}}F_{\mathrm{ion}},
\end{align}
where $k_{\mathrm{div}}$ is supplied. Beam power is checked through the
independent forms
\begin{equation}
  P_{\mathrm{beam}} =
  \sum_z m_{\mathrm{Xe}}\dot N_zv_z^2/2 = V_d I_b.
\end{equation}
The implementation reports explicitly bounded anode-to-beam,
thruster-electrical-to-beam, and PPU-input-to-beam efficiencies; it does not
emit an ambiguous total efficiency.
\EvidenceClaim{CLM-004}{Python and Warp share canonical preprocessing and then
evaluate the same ledgered relations through cross-backend implementations.}
The equation ledger is the normative source for domains and numerical edge
policies.

\subsection{Multi-fidelity optimization protocol}

Physics levels L0--L3 and campaign information sources F0--F3 are related by a
mapping recorded in each evaluation manifest; the labels are not silently
treated as synonyms. The committed campaign policy currently names F0
corrected-analytical, F1 fields/reduced, F2 hybrid, and F3 PIC/experiment.
\EvidenceClaim{CLM-009}{The committed campaign policy defines an eight-variable,
four-objective constrained record, asynchronous pending-aware ask/tell, typed
failures, immutable identities, bounded retries, equivalent-F3 planning cost,
and same-context Pareto comparisons; its benchmark results field remains
null.}

The acquisition portfolio is specified as constrained qLogNEHVI, MORBO-style
trust regions, and constrained qLogNParEGO. This is a policy, not a completed
benchmark. Publication requires common initial observations, equivalent-cost
checkpoints, a frozen F3-verified reference point and normalization, repeated
campaign seeds, confidence intervals, and identical failure and constraint
handling across candidate and baseline algorithms. The first executed campaign
on the corrected physics, an F0-only robust optimisation of the L0 model under a
declared closure, is admitted separately in Section~\ref{sec:mdo-l0}, and its
successor over the catalogue of screened designs in Section~\ref{sec:mdo-l0-v2};
both share the policy's fairness rules (common initial designs, equal budgets,
identical constraint handling, repeated seeds) but carry no F3 verification and
do not populate the benchmark field.

\section{Verification, validation, and uncertainty protocol}

The protocol follows a staged evidence hierarchy:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Code verification:} unit and property tests, manufactured
        solutions, analytic limits, binary64 edge cases, independent backend
        tests where available, cross-backend parity, and schema rejection tests.
  \item \textbf{Solution verification:} residual closure, mesh/time-step/domain
        refinement where discretization exists, repeated-run determinism or
        stochastic convergence, and numerical uncertainty.
  \item \textbf{Model-form assessment:} explicit discrepancy terms for omitted
        physics; no absorption of model discrepancy into emulator variance.
  \item \textbf{Validation:} preregistered comparisons with measurements,
        including instrument uncertainty, operating-condition uncertainty,
        facility effects, and withheld validation cases.
  \item \textbf{Prediction/UQ:} propagate aleatory and epistemic terms
        separately, report calibration and coverage diagnostics, and limit
        predictions to a declared applicability domain.
\end{enumerate}

For optimization, train/validation partitions are grouped by design so that
fidelities and replicates cannot leak across partitions. Promotion requires
feasibility, model calibration, out-of-distribution and invariant guardrails,

\section{Consistency of the four-cell power balance}
\label{sec:four-cell-closure}

The optimisation campaign of Section~\ref{sec:mdo-l0} evaluated a declared
closure rather than the corrected four-cell discharge solver because a probe of

\texttt{GATE-FOUR-CELL-CLOSURE-V1} (analysis revision \ClosureEvidenceRevision).
The object is the corrected four-cell power-balance ledger of the rebuild, a
system of residual rows in the cell potentials, electron temperatures,
ionisation source currents, electron, ion and cusp-ion currents that descends
from the reduced HEMP-thruster model of Kornfeld, Koch and Harmann
\cite{Kornfeld2007} and that the legacy design study solved with
\texttt{lsqnonlin} \cite{Muffatti2017}. Write $J_{e,k}=j_{e,k}(1-p_k)$ for the

mutually consistent and are parametrized by the four potentials) into the global
power row leaves
\begin{equation}
  R_{\FccGlobalRowIndex} = \FccAnodeFallCoefficient\,\bigl(J_{e,3}+I_4\bigr)

  \label{eq:four-cell-closed-form}
\end{equation}
where the first term is the anode-fall term and the second is the recombination
energy of the ions lost to the three interior cusps. Every cell potential,
temperature and ion-current term of the power expressions cancels in the
substitution. The gate admits the derivation and its numerical verification as

statement about the physical thruster, it accepts no correction of the ledger,

\section{Discussion}

The statements in this section are interpretation. They are bounded to the
admitted evidence, are not part of any preregistered estimand, and carry no
evidentiary weight beyond the registered claims they rest on.

\paragraph{Wall loss is not a mirror process in the qualified field, and that
statement is field-specific.}
The legacy design chain closes its cusp-loss estimate with a magnetic-mirror
acceptance-angle relation, the mirror-ratio and angular-arrival expressions of
the original study that its cusp-probability routine evaluates
\cite{Muffatti2017}.
\EvidenceClaim{CLM-017}{Because \WlfPooledReflected{} of the \WlfPooledTrials{}
integrated electrons reflected before reaching a boundary, the magnetic-mirror
picture of wall loss used by the original design study is not supported for
this field and design within the collisionless prescribed-field test-particle
model: the outcome is a split between wall capture and axial escape that is
decided almost entirely by the launch cell.} No mirror-formula loss estimate is
therefore published from this campaign, and the result says nothing about
collisional or self-consistent electron transport, in which scattering could
repopulate directions that the collisionless orbits never revisit. The
screening of Section~\ref{sec:wall-loss-geometry-screening} now bounds how far
that statement reaches.
\EvidenceClaim{CLM-052}{The mirror-picture statement of
Section~\ref{sec:wall-loss-v4} is scoped to the field it was made in: the
collisionless campaign on the qualified divergent-exit field recorded
\WlfPooledReflected{} reflections, whereas the screening of
Section~\ref{sec:wall-loss-geometry-screening} recorded reflections in every one
of its \WlgDesignsWithReflections{} linear-vacuum sweep designs
(\WlgReflectionShareAll{} of \WlgOrbitCount{} orbits) and saturated only
\WlgCellsSaturatedOne{} of \WlgDesignCells{} design-cells, so the absence of
reflections and the all-or-nothing per-cell pattern are properties of that
design and field within the collisionless model, not of the model or of the
design space; whether a reflection means confinement in any of these fields is
not decided by collisionless test particles. Read as interpretation, the
screening supplies, at screening tier and under its label, the
geometry-to-wall-loss map that the optimisation campaign of
Section~\ref{sec:mdo-l0} lacked; the design-dependent optimisation that consumes
it is admitted in Section~\ref{sec:mdo-l0-v2} under a declared closure, and the
geometry-to-performance link is realised there only as a closure-dependent
ranking at screening tier, not as a map from these fields to the operating-point
model.}

\paragraph{The multi-cell wall-cusp topology was undemonstrated under the
frozen definition and is a geometric property of the screened fields under the
literature definition.}
The legacy parameterisation presumes that consecutive wall cusps partition the
channel into confinement cells, and the original study's cusp-probability
closure is evaluated on that presumption \cite{Muffatti2017}. The admitted
screening studies of Section~\ref{sec:topology-screening} bear on it under one

the literature's \cite{Gildea2012,Lewerentz2023,Kornfeld2007}.
\EvidenceClaim{CLM-028}{Under the frozen cusp and cell definitions of the
admitted screening, the four-cell wall-cusp topology targeted by the original
design parameterisation was not shown stable for any of \FcnEvaluated{}
preregistered candidates, and \TchStableEligibleCusps{} stable eligible cusps
and \TchStableEligibleCells{} stable eligible cells were found across
\TchEvaluated{} characterised designs; those nulls hold under those definitions
and are not proof that no such design exists. The frozen definition, a
wall-side X-type vector null registered at the stage midplanes, was
non-standard for a periodic-permanent-magnet stack: the literature places the
point cusps on the axis, where each ring-cusp separatrix meets the centreline,
and the wall cusp where that separatrix meets the wall, at the magnet ends
rather than the stage midplanes. Under the literature definition the same
linear-vacuum fields carry the multi-cell wall-cusp topology as a geometric
property (Section~\ref{sec:cusp-topology}): \CtvSweepNMinusOne{} of
\CtvSweepCount{} sweep designs carry one wall cusp fewer than magnet stages,
\CtvSweepFourWallCusps{} carry exactly four wall cusps (fraction

remains open, and whether any cell confines a plasma is not a topology question
and is not decided here.} The accepted sweep's axis cusps are sampled sign

the separatrices they emit. What an accepted field-resolved manifest

topology of these equivalent-current fields survives permanent-magnet and iron
material models; the single P2 row of the topology screening is one
finite-element field with iron and not a sensitivity test. The wall-loss
campaign and the geometry screening answer neither question: their launch cells
are protocol positions in the channel, not the separatrix-bounded cells of the
catalogue, and their per-cell patterns describe where those positions sit
relative to the dielectric end, not a cell structure of the plasma.

\section{Limitations}

The L0 result is driven by hypothetical inputs and external closure
fractions. No measured-thruster comparison, geometric field response, plasma
chemistry, sheath or secondary-emission wall model, cathode model, thermal
model, erosion model, or facility correction is active. The admitted wall-loss
campaign is a collisionless test-particle result in one prescribed field; it
supplies no wall-interaction closure to any performance model, its coupling
export has been consumed only by the verifying consumer of the geometry
screening as a labelled reference row and by no plasma or performance model,
and its pooled fraction is a design average rather than a loss rate. The
admitted wall-loss geometry screening integrates collisionless test particles
in re-solved linear-vacuum screening fields that are not P2-qualified: its
dataset is screening input under its label, its refined-field diagnostic exists
for four representative designs only, its launch cells are protocol positions,
its geometry associations are observations of one launch design and not a
design rule, and no surrogate consuming the dataset is admitted (both surrogates
fitted to it were rejected on their predeclared gates); the one optimisation that
consumes it is the catalogue campaign of Section~\ref{sec:mdo-l0-v2}, admitted
under a declared closure. The admitted topology-screening studies use
linear-vacuum equivalent-current fields with a single mesh per design and
frozen cusp and cell definitions; their nulls hold under those definitions and
that field model only, those definitions differ from the literature's (see the
topology screening of Section~\ref{sec:cusp-topology}), they carry no
material-aware, plasma or performance content, and the sweep's mirror ratios
are screening quantities rather than confinement predictions. The admitted
cusp topology screening evaluates the literature definition on the same
linear-vacuum fields (a single mesh per design plus one refined map for
stability) and on one P2-qualified finite-element field whose iron sensitivity
was not tested: its cusps and cells are geometric properties of prescribed
field maps, its mirror ratios are field ratios and never probabilities, it
decides nothing about whether a cell confines a plasma, and its catalogue is a
consumer contract under its labels that no admitted consumer has yet
ingested. The admitted geometry sweep into the HEMP-like regime is a field-only
screening on the same linear-vacuum fields: the soft-iron pole pieces its
geometry declares are vacuum in the field, its Koch ratios are field ratios of
those fields and never probabilities, its preregistered hypothesis is reported at
its recorded outcome and was not confirmed as predicted, the material-aware
confirmation its protocol queues for every design with $r_w/L$ above one half was
not run, and none of its HEMP-like designs is a design recommendation. The legacy end-to-end workflow remains
unreproducible because source inputs and dependencies are missing. The admitted
optimisation campaign is F0-only: it optimises the L0 model under a declared,
unvalidated closure and declared priors, its hypervolume and paired comparisons
hold for the recorded budget, seeds and model alone, its robust constraint
enforces the worst sampled case rather than the worst case over the prior's
support, it carries no F3 verification and no surrogate diagnostics beyond the
recorded fits, and the campaign policy's benchmark field remains null. The
admitted catalogue campaign is F0-only as well: its design space is a discrete
catalogue of screened designs whose wall-hit counts come from linear-vacuum
fields that are not P2-qualified, its closure identifies a collisionless
test-particle wall-hit probability with a per-cusp survival factor by
declaration, its fronts and its design ranking are closure-dependent and hold
for the recorded budget, seeds and model alone, three seeds support counts and
never significance, its robust constraint enforces the worst sampled case, its
Bayesian runs explored the categorical design index unevenly across seeds, and
it carries no F3 verification. The
admitted four-cell consistency result is a statement about an equation set: it
establishes that the corrected ledger has no admissible root for positive
interior cusp probabilities and where its solutions lie, not that the ledger or
the proposed correction describes the thruster; the correction stays
unaccepted, the model has no potential closure, and the checker recomputes the
closed-form check, the continuation ladder, the anode-only closures, the
published-state misfit and one relaxed root, whereas the differential-evolution
and random-start searches and the eighty-case probe are documented values read
from the analysis document and the frozen protocol. The reading that the legacy
performance values were residual-floor artefacts is interpretation, and no value
of the unavailable legacy run is recomputed. The
current batch does not supply measurement comparison or calibrated model
discrepancy.

\section{Conclusion}

The updated-paper workstream converts an incomplete legacy study into an
auditable sequence of claims. At this evidence boundary, the accepted results
are limited to the strict L0 manifest, the typed manifest of the
collisionless test-particle wall-loss campaign, the three typed manifests
of the field-only topology screening admitted at their recorded outcomes and
the typed manifest of the robust optimisation campaign on the L0 model admitted
as optimiser evidence under a declared closure, the typed manifest of the
four-cell power-balance consistency analysis admitted as an analytic result
whose verification the checker recomputes, the typed manifest of the
wall-loss geometry screening admitted as a screening dataset under its label,
the typed manifest of the catalogue optimisation campaign over those
screened designs admitted as optimiser evidence under a declared closure whose
design ranking is closure-dependent, and the typed manifest of the cusp
topology screening admitted as a geometric screening of prescribed field maps
under the literature cusp definition, and the typed manifest of the geometry
sweep into the HEMP-like regime admitted as a field-only screening at its recorded
hypothesis outcome,
with their registered claim text and generated tables, accompanied by a
technical audit and campaign policy. Three of those results bear on the legacy design chain, and the
Discussion reads them together as the reason it cannot be reproduced from its
equations; that reading is interpretation, and the pivot it motivated, declared
closures and kinetic simulation, is admitted here only as far as the two
optimisation campaigns' declared closures. The topology screening moves the
multi-cell picture from an undemonstrated presumption under a frozen
definition to a geometric property of the screened fields under the
literature's; whether those cells confine a plasma remains outside every
admitted gate. The widened sweep places the legacy design box against the HEMP
design criterion and finds the criterion unreachable there in the screening
field, and reachable only at wall-radius-to-pitch ratios the legacy
parameterisation never sampled; whether those ratios survive a material-aware
field is the confirmation the protocol queues and this paper does not report.
The stronger inferences prohibited by the claim matrix remain outside scope.
Manifest-controlled L1--L3 sections make those future contributions visible
without presenting them as completed work.
